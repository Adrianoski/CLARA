ARXIV DATASET
=============

The validation corpus chosen is armanc/scientific_papers, a dataset
published on Hugging Face and originally built by Cohan et al. (2018) for
the long-document scientific summarization benchmark. The dataset collects
papers extracted from arXiv (the "arxiv" configuration used here) and from
PubMed OpenAccess ("pubmed" configuration, not used in this work), and is
typically employed in the literature to evaluate abstractive summarization
models on documents of considerable length. For this study, a sample of
2,000 papers was extracted from the "train" split of the arxiv
configuration, chosen without topical filtering: the resulting sample
therefore heterogeneously covers the areas most typically represented on
arXiv, in particular astrophysics, particle and condensed-matter physics,
pure and applied mathematics, and to a lesser extent computer science and
other quantitative disciplines.

Each dataset record consists of three text fields: the full body of the
paper (article), the abstract (abstract), and the list of section titles
(section_names). A characteristic relevant to preprocessing is that the
body text is not the original PDF, but a version derived from the LaTeX
source in which formulas and equations have been replaced by placeholder
tokens (e.g. @xmath12) and bibliographic references by the @xcite token;
the text is also entirely lowercase and shows non-standard spacing around
punctuation, inherited from the tokenization process used to prepare the
original dataset. This characteristic made a dedicated cleaning step
necessary before keyword extraction for semantic routing (see Section 3),
since such placeholders would otherwise tend to be extracted as spurious
distinctive terms.

Starting from this text corpus, the pipeline applies adaptive chunking
("paper" profile, target width of about 800 characters per chunk) and
computes a dense embedding for each chunk using the multilingual model
BAAI/bge-m3. The result is an index of about 143,000 chunks, subsequently
organized into semantic clusters (called SLMs, Semantic Language Model /
cluster) via a batch clustering algorithm (MiniBatchKMeans), with an
additional recursive splitting step for clusters exceeding a maximum size
threshold, so as to obtain clusters of more homogeneous size than a plain
single-pass K-Means would produce. On this 2,000-paper / 143,086-chunk
corpus, this procedure produces 204 clusters (mean size 701 chunks, median
655, largest 1,298, smallest 2) — see "Number of clusters produced" below
for the full breakdown and its implications for scalability.


3. IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS / HYPERPARAMETERS (fills R3's requested numbers)
============================================================================

This section reports, explicitly, every numeric parameter governing
clustering, routing and retrieval, as requested by reviewer R3. All values
are read directly from the implementation, not estimated.

3.1 Chunking profiles.
The chunking step is adaptive: the document type is heuristically detected
and a type-specific (chunk_size, chunk_overlap) pair is applied, using a
recursive character splitter with separators ["\n\n", "\n", ".", " ", ""].

  document type   chunk_size (chars)   chunk_overlap (chars)
  paper                    800                   100
  book                    1500                   150
  technical               1200                   200
  generic                 1500                   150

The arXiv corpus uses the "paper" profile throughout (800 / 100), since
every record is a scientific-paper body by construction.

3.2 Clustering: two regimes, and why.
Two distinct clustering mechanisms exist in the implementation, used in
different regimes:

  (a) Incremental threshold-based assignment (the general CLARA mechanism,
      used for streaming / single-document ingestion). Each new chunk is
      assigned to the existing SLM whose centroid has the highest cosine
      similarity, provided that similarity is >= tau_rel (the assignment
      threshold); otherwise a new SLM is created, seeded by that chunk.
      Two SLMs whose centroids have cosine similarity >= the merge
      threshold are subsequently merged, keeping the larger of the two.

        tau_rel (assignment threshold)  default = 0.65  (code: router.py,
                                          assign_chunk(), parameter
                                          "threshold")
        merge threshold                 default = 0.90  (code: router.py,
                                          merge_close_slms(), parameter
                                          "threshold")

  (b) Batch clustering with a recursive size cap (used specifically for
      the arXiv corpus). Chunk embeddings are clustered in one pass with
      MiniBatchKMeans into an initial target number of clusters; every
      resulting cluster whose size exceeds tau_size is recursively
      re-clustered internally until all clusters satisfy the cap.

        target initial cluster count (K)   150   (code: recluster.py,
                                                   N_CLUSTERS)
        tau_size (max chunks per SLM)      1300  (code: recluster.py,
                                                   MAX_CHUNKS_PER_SLM)

  Why two regimes: on this corpus, regime (a) was tested at tau_rel = 0.55
  and tau_rel = 0.75. At 0.55, 98.9% of all chunks collapsed into a single
  SLM (100,431 of 101,542 chunks then indexed): the mostly-boilerplate,
  LaTeX-derived text of heterogeneous arXiv papers is similar enough in
  embedding space that a permissive threshold merges nearly everything
  into one attractor. At 0.75, the opposite failure occurred: roughly
  13-14 SLMs were created per paper, extrapolating to ~27,000 SLMs over
  2,000 papers — far too many for the merge step (itself O(n^2) per merge
  found) to consolidate in tractable time, and too fragmented to be
  useful clusters. Regime (b) was adopted instead specifically for this
  large, topically heterogeneous corpus, and is documented here as a
  corpus-scale adaptation of the general CLARA clustering mechanism (a),
  which remains the default for the streaming/single-document case.

3.3 Relevance score.
Throughout the retrieval pipeline (routing, dense ranking, and clustering
similarity), the relevance score between any two embedded items is cosine
similarity: score(u, v) = (u . v) / (||u|| ||v||), computed on L2-normalized
embeddings from BAAI/bge-m3.

3.4 Keyword profile / routing parameters.
Each SLM's routing profile is built from its most distinctive keywords,
extracted with spaCy from the (LaTeX/arXiv-artifact-cleaned) text of its
member chunks, and weighted with a cross-cluster c-TF-IDF scheme: a
keyword's score in cluster c is its in-cluster count multiplied by
log((1 + N) / (1 + df)), where N is the total number of clusters and df is
the number of clusters in which the keyword appears at all. This penalizes
keywords generic across many clusters and rewards those distinctive of a
single cluster.

  CTFIDF_TOP_K (keywords retained per cluster profile)       25
  CTFIDF_MIN_COUNT (min in-cluster occurrences to be
                     eligible for the c-TF-IDF ranking)        3
  SLM_SUMMARY_EVERY_N (regime (a) only: chunks added
                        before an in-flight summary refresh)  20

Each retained keyword is embedded individually (not concatenated into a
single string); the cluster's routing score for a query is the mean of
the cosine similarities between the query embedding and the cluster's
keyword embeddings (an alternative, centroid-based routing mode, scoring
each cluster by cosine similarity to its centroid embedding rather than
its keyword profile, is also implemented and used as an ablation
baseline — see Results below for which mode produced the reported run).

3.5 Retrieval parameters.
  RRF_K (Reciprocal Rank Fusion constant)         60
  top-N SLM (clusters selected by routing)        run-dependent, see
                                                    Results section
  top-K (chunks passed to the generator after
         fusion)                                  run-dependent, see
                                                    Results section

3.6 Generation parameters.
  model            Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B
  decoding         greedy (do_sample = False)
  max new tokens   256
  prompt template  identical for StdRAG and CLARA (SLMAgent.build_messages)


4. BASELINE DEFINITION
========================

4.1 Definition of StdRAG.
StdRAG (Standard RAG) is not an external system but a controlled ablation
of CLARA with routing disabled. Every component of the pipeline is held
identical between StdRAG and CLARA except the search pool that is
retrieved from; this isolates the effect of semantic routing as the sole
independent variable in the comparison.

Specifically, StdRAG and CLARA share:
  - the same chunking pipeline and per-type profiles (Section 3.1);
  - the same embedding model (BAAI/bge-m3) for both indexing and query
    encoding;
  - the same underlying vector store (a ChromaDB collection);
  - the same hybrid retrieval mechanism: a dense ranking (cosine
    similarity, Section 3.3) and a lexical BM25 ranking, fused via
    Reciprocal Rank Fusion with the same constant RRF_K = 60 (Section
    3.5);
  - the same top-K cutoff applied to the fused ranking before the chunks
    are handed to the generator;
  - the same generative model and decoding parameters (Section 3.6),
    including an identical prompt template.

The single difference is the pool that the dense+BM25+RRF retrieval step
is run over. For CLARA, the pool is the union of the top-N SLM clusters
selected by semantic routing (a strict subset of the corpus, typically a
few percent of it — see the running example in Section 5). For StdRAG,
routing is disabled and the pool is instead the entire indexed corpus: all
~143,000 chunks, retrieved via an exact (flat, non-approximate) cosine
similarity computation over every embedding in the collection — i.e. an
exhaustive nearest-neighbour search, not an ANN index such as HNSW or
FAISS, so that StdRAG's retrieval quality is not itself limited by
approximation error and represents a genuine upper bound.

Because StdRAG and CLARA are identical in every respect except the search
pool, any difference observed between them in retrieval latency, pool
size, or answer quality can be attributed specifically to the effect of
semantic routing, rather than to confounding differences in chunking,
embedding, fusion, or generation.


NUMBER OF CLUSTERS PRODUCED
============================

On the 2,000-paper / 143,086-chunk arXiv sample, the batch clustering
procedure produces 204 final SLM clusters. Their size
distribution: mean 701 chunks, median 655, largest 1,298 chunks, smallest
2 chunks. This demonstrates the clustering step scaling from
the original single-document use case (tens of SLMs, one per book
chapter) to a 2,000-document / 143k-chunk heterogeneous corpus while
keeping every cluster within a bounded, near-uniform size — the property
that keeps downstream routing and retrieval costs predictable as the
corpus grows.


5. RESULTS FROM THE LATEST RUN (speedup)
==========================================

Configuration: top-N SLM = 15, top-K chunks = 5, generation context =
16,384 tokens, keyword-based routing (summary embedding, c-TF-IDF).

Retrieval speedup:
  - SLM-RAG   :  11.8x faster than StdRAG

Average pool queried per query:
  - StdRAG    : 143,086 chunks (100% of the corpus)
  - SLM-RAG   : 11779 chunks  

Average generation time:
  - StdRAG    : 2861 ms
  - SLM-RAG   : 2485 ms


6. ROUTING RUNNING EXAMPLE
============================

Below is a step-by-step walkthrough of the routing and retrieval flow for
a real benchmark query, with the values actually produced by the system
(204-SLM registry, keyword-based routing). The query concerns the content
of paper "paper_000219", about photon emission from quantum dots.

Query:
  "How does resonant excitation improve the spectral linewidth of photons
   emitted by a quantum dot?"

Step 1 — Query embedding.
The query is encoded with the same embedding model used at indexing time
(BAAI/bge-m3), producing a normalized dense vector q_emb in the same
vector space in which both the corpus chunks and the clusters' semantic
profiles live.

Step 2 — Comparison against the clusters' semantic profiles (routing).
Each SLM has a routing profile built from its most distinctive keywords
(Section 3.4). Each keyword in the profile is embedded individually; the
cluster's routing score is the mean of the cosine similarities between
q_emb and its keyword embeddings. Across the registry's 204 clusters, the
five highest scores obtained for this query were:

  rank  SLM            score    n. chunks  example keywords
  1     slm_c98064bb   0.3776   908        coulomb photon exchange,
                                            rayleigh scattering cross
                                            sections, photoelectron
  2     slm_d0d3f6c1   0.3750   922        radiofrequency, hf radio
                                            signal, wakefield
  3     slm_42cfc9c3   0.3684   749        laser linewidths, opo
                                            cavity linewidth,
                                            photoelectron
  4     slm_5ed7c2b7   0.3669   1078       nir image processing,
                                            gaia g band photometry
  5     slm_6d21655e   0.3657   510        quantum state engineering,
                                            photon quadrature,
                                            beamsplitter

Step 3 — Top-N selection and pool composition.
With top-N = 3, clusters slm_c98064bb, slm_d0d3f6c1 and slm_42cfc9c3 are
selected as the search pool for this query. Their union amounts to 2,579
chunks, roughly 1.8% of the entire corpus (143,086 chunks): this is the
subset that will actually be queried, instead of the full index as StdRAG
would do.

Step 4 — Hybrid retrieval within the pool.
Within the 2,579 selected chunks, the same hybrid retrieval scheme used by
StdRAG is run, but limited to the pool (Section 4.1): a dense ranking
(cosine similarity between q_emb and each pool chunk's embedding) and a
lexical BM25 ranking over the chunks' text, fused via Reciprocal Rank
Fusion (RRF, k = 60). The top-ranked chunk indeed comes from slm_c98064bb,
has cosine similarity 0.658 with the query, and its text is:

  "in our experiment, we begin with an optical characterization of the qds
   and observe a significantly reduced spectral linewidth of the emitted
   photons from a resonantly driven single qd compared with incoherent
   excitation methods..."

— a genuine excerpt from paper_000219, i.e. exactly the document the query
refers to: routing correctly directed the search toward the cluster that
held the relevant information, even though its keyword profile at first
glance ("coulomb photon exchange", "rayleigh scattering cross sections")
would not seem the most obvious match — the keywords nonetheless share the
vocabulary of photon physics, which is enough for the comparison in
embedding space to work correctly.

Step 5 — Answer generation.
The chunks selected in step 4 (up to top-K) are finally passed, in the
prompt, to the generative model (Qwen3.5-4B) together with the original
query, which produces the final answer based only on this restricted
context rather than on the entire corpus (Section 3.6).

The overall cost of the retrieval operation for this query is therefore
limited to 1.8% of the total corpus (2,579 chunks versus StdRAG's
143,086), while still correctly retrieving the chunk most relevant to the
question.
